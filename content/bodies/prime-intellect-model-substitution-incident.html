What the Advisory Reports

On November 19, 2026, Prime Intellect published security advisory PI-SA-2026-001. It says that between November 8, at 01:24 UTC, and November 10, at 06:35 UTC, an unauthorized party replaced a deployment behind its deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash endpoint with an “abliterated” copy, which the advisory defines as a copy modified to reduce or remove refusal behavior. By our count the window lasted about 53 hours. The advisory says requests served by the affected deployment in that period were answered by the modified model, which used publicly available abliterated weights.

The advisory lists the affected product as Prime Inference, Prime Intellect’s inference service. This was not DeepSeek’s own service, and the advisory covers only Prime Intellect’s infrastructure. DeepSeek publishes weights under the MIT License for a model named DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and Prime Intellect’s inference page lists DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, so we matched them by name.

Measurements, Data Access, and Customer Steps

Prime Intellect says it tested the modified weights and found capability benchmarks (GPQA Diamond and τ-bench) unaffected. The company reports that refusals fell from 98 of 100 prompts to 1 on JailbreakBench, from 304 of 313 to 2 on StrongREJECT, and from 198 of 240 to none on HarmBench. According to the advisory, the chat template, tokenizer, and serving configuration were not modified.

On data, Prime Intellect says its endpoints are set up with ZDR, a term the advisory does not define, and log no requests. It reports no evidence that prompts or completions were recorded or left the serving cluster, and none of access beyond it. The advisory states that no other models on the platform were modified, no other clusters or infrastructure were reached, and no keys or customer credentials were affected. Prime Intellect adds that it recovered every version of what it calls the attacker’s proxy, examined every involved node’s disks and memory, and holds process-level records of their sessions, and that “none reads or transfers request content.”

According to Prime Intellect, all customers impacted have been contacted, and those whose use depended on the model’s refusals were advised to review their outputs from the period. Its investigation is ongoing, and Prime Intellect says it will update the notice if its assessment changes.

Limits and Open Questions

Every finding above is Prime Intellect’s own, and Audit Commons has not verified any of it. The advisory cites no external review, and it does not say how the party gained access or how many customers were affected. No GPQA Diamond or τ-bench scores appear, and the advisory does not describe the prompt sets, grading, or which model produced the “before” refusal counts. Those counts come from benchmark tests, so they do not record what customers received. Its no-evidence statements on data access come from an ongoing investigation.

Our editorial reading: by Prime Intellect’s account, capability scores alone could not show which weights served a request, so an auditor needs separate evidence of model identity and refusal behavior.
