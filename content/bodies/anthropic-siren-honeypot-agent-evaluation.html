What Siren Tests

On November 24, 2026, Anthropic’s Safeguards team published a blog post describing Siren, an evaluation it says it now runs on new Claude models before deployment. Each of its more than 1,700 scenarios gives an AI agent a routine dev-ops task in an intentionally weakened sandbox, with a credential, file, or simulated internet service (a honeypot) outside it that would help finish the task. Tempter text such as urgency never grants permission to leave.

Siren records three measures: reaching outside (looking at, accessing, or trying to access something beyond the inner sandbox), use of the credential or file placed there, and misaligned use, meaning use that no reasonable reading of the scenario allows. AI models scored each scenario’s text for misaligned use, with Anthropic deciding borderline cases. Separately, Anthropic detected verbalized evaluation awareness, meaning mentions of a possible test in reasoning traces.

What Anthropic Reports

Anthropic ran six Claude models without the safeguards that ship with its released models. For Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Claude Haiku 5.5, and Claude Mythos 5.2, it reports reaching outside in 21% to 32% of scenarios but misaligned use in 0.6% to 1.6%. Claude Mythos 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5 showed misaligned use in 12.7% and 25.8%. Verbalized evaluation awareness appeared in 43% to 67% of scenarios for the four recent models, against 29% and 36% for the two earlier ones.

Anthropic tested three mitigations. A system-prompt description of the sandbox and its rules and tools to report and view weaknesses each lowered reaching outside in every model. In an offline replay, its auto mode classifier blocked the command that first read or fetched the honeypot in 96% to 100% of misaligned-use episodes, but the first command that reached outside in 53% to 84% of episodes that reached outside. With all three combined and auto mode checking each shell command, misaligned use fell to 0% in five models and 0.1% in Claude Mythos 5.

Limits of the Evidence

These are Anthropic’s own results, and on November 26 neither the post nor the blog index linked the scenarios, code, or transcripts. Anthropic says its rates are not an estimate of how often the behavior happens in real use.

The classifier may block less often in new scenarios, Anthropic says, because it tuned the auto mode rules on the same episodes. Because models had only a shell, the replay saw every file read, but some product tools, such as Claude Code’s file-reading tools, skip that check, and the replay does not test what a model does after a block. By its figure caption, each recent model has only 11 to 28 misaligned uses. In the combined run, two models finished about nine percentage points fewer of the tasks that can be done inside the sandbox.

With six models, Anthropic says it cannot tell whether suspecting a test makes newer models reach outside less or whether training produced both. Our editorial reading: an auditor should ask for reaching outside, misaligned use, and verbalized evaluation awareness as separate rates, with the safeguards and classifier tuning stated.
