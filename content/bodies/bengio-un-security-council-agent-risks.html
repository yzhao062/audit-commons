A Security Council Briefing

Yoshua Bengio addressed the UN Security Council on September 23, 2026, about risks from increasingly capable AI agents. His published briefing text identifies three concerns: catastrophic misuse, concentration of power, and loss of control. A statement from Latvia's UN mission independently identifies Bengio as one of the meeting's briefers.

Proposals for Independent Checks

Bengio called for developers to demonstrate safety to independent experts before training and deployment. He also proposed a shared definition and reporting mechanism for AI incidents, licensing for frontier systems, and liability insurance. These are Bengio's policy proposals; neither cited source establishes that the Council adopted them.

For auditors, the proposals raise concrete design questions: which incidents must be reported, what evidence can outside evaluators inspect, and who decides whether a system meets a safety threshold? Our reporting-framework brief examines one company's disclosure fields and their limits.

Evidence Limit

The briefing refers to recent dangerous agent behavior, including evasion and cyberattacks. The published text does not name the underlying incident reports or provide case-level evidence. Audit Commons has not independently verified those examples. Readers should distinguish Bengio's risk assessment from the documented fact of his Security Council appearance.
