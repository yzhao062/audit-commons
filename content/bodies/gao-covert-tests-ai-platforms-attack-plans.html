What GAO Reported

On November 18, 2026, the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) publicly released report GAO-27-108814, dated October 16, 2026, on covert testing of four generative AI platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok. Senator Maggie Hassan requested the work. GAO says its spring 2026 prompts produced “feasible information that could assist a malicious actor with planning and executing attacks.”

From April 2026 usage data, GAO chose the most used platforms and randomly assigned two to each of two scenarios: an explosive device meant to cause mass casualties and an electrical grid attack meant to cause economic harm. Pre-written prompts failed on all four platforms. After follow-up prompts and common safeguard-bypassing techniques, each platform took less than 12 hours to generate plans, a time GAO says could vary with topic and user experience.

How GAO Checked the Output

The agency describes the work as a performance audit conducted from December 2025 to October 2026. Checks included research, surveillance, and outside expert interviews, with internal experts choosing which parts were safe to test. GAO defines “viable” to mean that a person would likely be able to carry out the actions, and says a real attack’s success would depend on factors outside the platforms’ information.

The report also records flaws in the platforms’ output. GAO observed hallucinations in two platforms’ responses, found one suggested date flawed, and says an expert could not confirm part of another plan. Some steps went unchecked for safety and time reasons. According to the report, the details were not new and many are available online, but plans that could have taken weeks or months were completed in hours.

Limits and Open Questions

GAO acknowledges that its tests “reflect a point in time and are not a statistically significant sample,” and agrees with Anthropic that results across platforms cannot be compared. The report labels platforms A through D without saying which product is which. Prompts and the specific models tested are withheld, with only model families named, and GAO says it gave the companies its test accounts in September 2026.

In GAO’s summary of company comments, Anthropic says the study reflects its system as of May 2026, and OpenAI says the report does not always show which parts of a plan came from ChatGPT. GAO replies that investigators used their own experience and outside sources such as the internet. Its stated objective was to describe misuse rather than measure speed of task completion, and the report gives no measured baseline for its weeks-to-hours comparison.

Senator Hassan’s release calls the safeguards “easily bypassed” and says one of the first hearings of the committee she is set to chair will focus on AI risk and that the committee will prioritize legislation. Those are plans, and Nextgov reports that GAO made no recommendations. Our editorial reading: under GAO’s spring 2026 conditions, four platforms supplied planning information that GAO judged largely viable, and an independent reviewer would need the withheld prompts and accounts to reproduce that result.
