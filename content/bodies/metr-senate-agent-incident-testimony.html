Public Evidence Depends on Access

METR President Chris Painter's September 30 Senate testimony revisits the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident. He says better public visibility into frontier capabilities, incidents, and safeguards is valuable under almost any policy choice. METR's brief investigation with Redwood Research relied on voluntary access and was scoped to agent behavior, reasoning, and collaboration. It was not focused on OpenAI's cybersecurity measures or organizational practices.

Reading an Independent Investigation

For readers evaluating an audit claim, our editorial recommendation is to check the access agreement, questions investigated, redactions, and evidence released. A useful report should make those boundaries easy to find.

Painter explicitly avoids endorsing a particular policy agenda. The testimony is evidence for policy debate, not an enacted reporting mandate. Compare its access limits with the White House accord's external-assessment commitments: a promise of review still leaves the review's scope and public evidence to be established.
