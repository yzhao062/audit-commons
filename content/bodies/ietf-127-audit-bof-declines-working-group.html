What the AUDIT BoF Considered

On November 17, 2026, at IETF 127 in San Francisco, the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) held a 90-minute BoF, a session to decide whether to charter a working group, on AUDIT (Agent Use of Delegation and Interaction Traceability). The draft charter proposes post-hoc auditing of agent actions across administrative and trust domains, and says audit information and its integrity “must be verifiable by a party that trusts neither the agent nor its operator.”

Poll Results, Objections, and the Decision

Meetecho polls with more than 250 people present backed the venue and rejected the scope. Among respondents, 113 said yes and 28 no on whether the IETF is the right place, while 33 said yes and 119 no on whether the initial scope is correct. A majority also answered no on forming a working group and on whether the problem is well understood, solvable, and useful to solve. Even so, a majority said they would help write or review drafts.

The session notes record speakers questioning whether the scope is too broad, how records would protect privacy, which threat model applies, and who may read or verify them.

Security Area Director Christopher Inacio is recorded as saying the scope needs much more refinement. He saw gaps in motivating cases for the privacy design and for how a verifier asserts integrity, and said trust domains seem largely underspecified. The notes list the outcome as no working group now: the charter returns to its proponents, and a second BoF would evaluate it again. AUDIT’s group page reads “Not chartered yet.” The chairs told the mailing list it would reopen after two weeks to prepare for a potential second BoF, subject to IESG approval.

Related Drafts and Limits

One of the four drafts we covered in October, draft-thaha-scitt-agent-execution-evidence, was among four individual drafts that the SCITT working group polled on November 18 (Pacific time). Datatracker’s poll record shows 1 yes and 22 no on whether attendees had read all four. The SCITT notes say the chair called them heavily overlapping and asked for volunteers to consolidate them into one document. None of the other three drafts we covered was among them.

On November 20, Datatracker recorded approval of a -00 working group draft for AGENTPROTO (agent communication protocols). The draft says accountability and auditing of agent actions are out of scope for that document and carries an editor’s note to reference IETF audit work once it progresses.

The minutes posted to the session page on November 21 reproduce these working notes, which still mark several names and figures as uncertain. Their chair readouts differ by a few votes from the Datatracker record, and the area directors decide how much the polls matter. We did not watch the recordings. Our editorial reading: until a charter settles who may read agent audit records and what a verifier attests, treat each draft record format as its authors’ proposal.
