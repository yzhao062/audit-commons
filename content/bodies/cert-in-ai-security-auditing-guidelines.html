Risk Tiers Set Audit Independence

The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) has published Artificial Intelligence (AI) Security Auditing Guidelines, version 1.0, dated November 4, 2026. The 107-page PDF scores each AI system from 1 to 4 on eight inherent-risk factors "without taking credit for claimed control effectiveness." Capabilities are to be "established rather than inferred from policy statements" (page 23). Tier 1 permits self-assessment. Tier 2 adds an independent audit by a "CERT-In empanelled auditing organisation" at least annually. Tiers 3 and 4 call for independent audits by such organizations, with continuous monitoring at Tier 4 (pages 25 and 26).

What Auditors Check in Agents

Agents "shall" have documented identities, approved tools, permission boundaries, and action limits, without automatically inheriting permissions from users, tools, or other agents. Tool calls, delegated tasks, approvals, and external actions needed to reconstruct the execution chain are to be logged and protected against unauthorized modification (Section 9.7, page 66). Auditors are told not to rely solely on the organization's self-assessed tier (page 37), to sample agent tool calls and autonomous actions where applicable (page 38), and to test whether approval gates can be bypassed through alternate interfaces (page 80).

Status and Limits

The PDF says a 2025 national policy framework provides "the policy authority" for its audit requirements (page 102) and does not cite a statutory provision making it binding. CERT-In's Directions page lists 2022 directions, not this document. We have not assessed its legal effect. It reports no audit results.
