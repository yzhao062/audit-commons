# Editorial Images

`content/media.json` is the curated image registry. It maps article slugs and resource IDs to local assets, independently of the Awesome catalog importer. Catalog imports must not rewrite this file.

## News Leads

Every `news` article requires a relevant registered lead image with bilingual alt text and caption. Choose an image that lets readers recognize the story's subject. In order of preference (maintainer instructions, October 28 and November 9, 2026):

1. Authentic photographs that identify the relevant companies, institutions, people, events, places, products, or tasks.
2. Genuine product or task interface screenshots, labeled as screenshots in both captions.
3. The authentic official logo of the company, product, or institution the story concerns, with its actual rights and governing terms checked (see [Rights](#rights)). Prefer the owner's own source, such as its website, press kit, or brand page. A faithful copy hosted elsewhere, such as on Wikimedia Commons, qualifies only after it has been checked against the owner's published branding. Use the file unmodified, record its exact download URL and the rights statement and trademark terms that actually apply, and do not assume a license carries over from the host, the owner, or a repository. Imply no affiliation or endorsement.
4. Published paper charts, report tables, and screenshots of report text. These support evidence in the article; they are not a default lead.

Title cards, title-only screenshots, generic decoration, pure text-box diagrams, and recreated or altered marks do not qualify. AI-generated or other generated illustrations are never a default or fallback: use one only at the user's explicit request or approval, and disclose AI generation in both language captions. Without that approval, keep searching sources and report the missing real image. The build rejects news articles without a registered image; editors must visually review relevance and recognizability. Images remain optional for other article types. Do not invent a project logo or represent an illustration as documentary photography. Archive photographs carry their original dates in captions. A project screenshot or published chart is illustrative source material, not an experiment performed by Audit Commons.

## Adding an Image

1. Verify the source page, depicted subject, relevance to the entry, and the terms that actually govern the asset: its own license or rights statement and the publisher's site, logo, trademark, or government copyright policy. Prefer the project's own repository or documentation, licensed photographs, and government material whose copyright policy permits reuse.
2. Store source assets unmodified in `assets/media/`. When a vector figure must be rendered from a PDF, or a source screenshot captured, record the page, resolution or viewport and scale, and exact crop in `changes`; never add or redraw content. Approved original editorial illustrations also live there; identify Audit Commons as their creator, link the evidence they explain in the article, and record their canonical article URL, public asset URL, and actual reuse status. Without a reuse license, name the copyright holder, link the governing terms, and describe the use as an attributed editorial excerpt; do not use material whose applicable published terms explicitly forbid reuse or require permission that is not recorded (see [Rights](#rights)). Set `license_pending: true` only when a reuse license is actually pending; the credit links to the rights statement while structured data omits a license claim. Record source and download URLs, credit, rights or license link (the governing terms, never a report or file download link), retrieval date, SHA-256, intrinsic dimensions, descriptive alt text, and a factual caption. Retain applicable license and notice files under `assets/media/licenses/`.
3. Map the article slug or resource ID to the asset key in the registry. Use `photo`, `logo`, `screenshot`, or `figure` to select presentation. The build rejects broken mappings, missing credits, missing assets, digest changes, and active or externally dependent SVG content. Embedded PNG/JPEG data in an upstream SVG is supported.
4. Build and check desktop and mobile layouts. Display marks and scientific figures without cropping or distorting them. The full local image is accessible from article figures; resource images open their resource. Avoid large downloads and do not add tracking pixels or hotlinked image dependencies.

Article captions show credits and rights links. Resource cards place these in their provenance details. Compact homepage thumbnails link to the corresponding attributed article or resource. Informative images have alt text and fixed intrinsic dimensions; images below the lead are lazy-loaded. Article images also supply structured-data metadata. Raster photographs, figures, and logos at least 600 pixels wide can supply social previews; screenshots, SVGs, and smaller images retain the site's raster social-preview fallback. This keeps scores inside example interfaces from being mistaken for editorial findings in caption-free share cards. Article images are not enlarged beyond their intrinsic dimensions.

The initial set is about 3.8 MB in total. Browser verification measured roughly 2.4 MB of media after scrolling the full homepage; lazy loading reduces the initial load. Images are currently preserved at their sourced dimensions. Future image additions should consider appropriately sized upstream thumbnails or explicitly documented derivatives, rather than increasing full-page transfer indefinitely.

## Rights

Each image retains its own rights. Repository licenses are recorded from the upstream project; they do not grant ownership of trademarks. Logos and project banners identify the subject of a news story or editorial listing and imply no affiliation or endorsement. An official source, or a caption that calls the use editorial identification, does not grant reuse rights. When published terms that govern the asset explicitly forbid reuse or require permission, and the registry records neither that permission nor an explicit reuse grant that applies, choose a verified Creative Commons or public-domain real photograph or another licensed authentic asset instead. A mark with independently verified copyright-free status, such as a public-domain text logo, and no such restriction needs no separate owner license; record that status and its source. Do not generate an image as a workaround, and do not ask the user by default when such a licensed replacement is available. Upstream copyright and license notices are retained alongside the assets. These image-specific permissions do not set the license of the Audit Commons website.

The initial set includes licensed archive photographs of Dario Amodei and Sam Altman, NIST campus photography, the METR mark, an author-supplied optstop figure, Inspect's documented log viewer, and project marks or previews for selected resources. The registry is the authoritative per-image provenance record.
